# The Eight-Stage Learning Engine

The pedagogy inside a single session. Every activity in Today's Mission runs this loop once, which is why a mission of four activities is four passes through it rather than one long conversation.

This document says *why* each stage exists. Where a stage became a mechanism, [`session-engine.md`](./session-engine.md) says how: stage 4 is the opportunity-ratio evaluator and the `common_errors` match, stage 5 is the retry trigger and scaffold ladder, stage 6 writes to the `reflection` table, stage 7 is the mastery update, and stage 8 is the retrievability model plus the `sameTemplateAsLastTimeFor` penalty that forces the new context. Where the two disagree, the engine wins.

## Stage 1 — Stimulus

Purpose

Provide meaningful input.

Possible forms

* Image  
* Short audio  
* Conversation  
* Story  
* Video clip  
* Situation  
* Real-life object

Example

(Image)

A child dropping ice cream.

---

## Stage 2 — Comprehension

Before speaking, confirm the learner understands the stimulus.

Examples

"What happened?"

"What do you notice?"

"Who is crying?"

Children always understand before they produce.

---

## Stage 3 — Production

Now they speak.

Depending on level:

Beginner

> Name three things.

Intermediate

> Describe the picture.

Advanced

> Explain why the child reacted this way.

Notice that the stimulus stays the same while the task changes.

---

## Stage 4 — Immediate Feedback

This is where AI shines.

Instead of simply saying

"Wrong."

It can separate feedback into layers.

Vocabulary

"You used *sad*. A more natural word here is *upset*."

Grammar

"You should say *fell*, not *falled*."

Pronunciation

"Your stress should be on the first syllable."

Fluency

"You paused for several seconds."

---

## Stage 5 — Retry

One correction rarely changes long-term behavior.

The learner immediately retries.

This mirrors how children often repeat corrected language almost instantly.

---

## Stage 6 — Reflection (Missing from your model)

This is surprisingly powerful.

Ask one simple question.

"What changed?"

or

"What was your mistake?"

The learner verbalizes the correction.

That strengthens encoding.

---

## Stage 7 — Store Weaknesses

Don't store everything.

Store only things that matter.

Examples

User:

incorrect:  
"I goed."

correct:  
"I went."

Category:  
Past tense

Difficulty:  
Easy

Attempts:  
2

Last seen:  
Today

Or

Pronunciation

Word:  
comfortable

Problem:  
Stress

Your database slowly becomes a personalized model of the learner.

---

## Stage 8 — Spaced Retrieval in a New Context

This is where memory becomes durable.

Suppose yesterday the learner practiced

"I spilled my coffee."

Tomorrow

Show a different image.

Milk spilling.

Ask

"What happened?"

The learner now says

"He spilled his milk."

Same grammar.

Different context.
