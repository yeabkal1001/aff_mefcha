I actually think this is one of the best things we can do. 

One thing I've noticed about hackathon judges is that they don't remember features—they remember **stories** . 

If you simply demo: 

"Here's our AI." 

They'll forget it. 

If instead you say: 

"Meet Hana. She's an 18-year-old Ethiopian student who just got accepted to university. She's terrified of speaking English during presentations..." 

Now the judges become emotionally invested. 

I would actually build the entire demo around **one person's journey** .

# **This Leads to Your Biggest Product Differentiator** 

I think we should organize the platform around **Life Paths** , not just courses. 

When a learner signs up, they don't choose "English Level" as the primary experience—they choose **why they want to improve** . 

Examples: 

- 🎓 **University Success** — Classroom discussions, presentations, group projects, speaking with professors. 

- 💼 **Job Interview Success** — Introductions, behavioral interviews, technical interviews, salary discussions. 

- 🏨 **Hospitality & Tourism** — Guest interactions, reservations, complaint handling, concierge conversations. 

- ✈ **Study Abroad** — Visa interviews, airport conversations, orientation, academic communication. 

- 💻 **Software Engineering** — Stand-up meetings, explaining projects, code reviews, technical presentations. 

- 🩺 **Healthcare** — Patient communication, teamwork, explaining procedures (future expansion). 

The AI then builds every conversation, vocabulary list, correction, and progression around that path. 

That transforms the product from **"an app that teaches English"** into **"an AI coach that prepares people for the next stage of their lives."** From both a product and business perspective, that's a much stronger story to present to hackathon judges.

# **Appendix — What the story is actually doing**

Every beat above is produced by the engine in [`../architecture/session-engine.md`](../architecture/session-engine.md). Nothing in the demo is scripted. This table is for us, not for the judges, and it's what we point at if a technical judge asks "is any of this real?"

| Story beat | System mechanism |
| --- | --- |
| Onboarding never asks for a level | Life Path, L1, study field and daily budget are the generator's inputs; level is measured, not declared |
| The four-minute assessment | Three sessions (`EX001`, `EX007`, `EX009`) seed baseline mastery with `evidence_count = 1` |
| "These numbers move quickly at first" | Update rate is `α = max(0.15, 1/(1+evidence_count))`, so early evidence moves the estimate hard and later evidence refines it |
| Today's Mission, 20 minutes, four activities | A2 row of the learning-load table: 4 sessions, 2–3 new concepts, 2 reviews |
| The mission has one story, not four drills | Day Plan themed by the Life Path context substitution: "Introducing yourself" → "Introducing yourself to a university class" |
| "I am study" is caught | Matched deterministically against `G006.01.common_errors` tag `missing_participle` |
| The Amharic correction | Addis AI translation and TTS; the English model sentence is spoken by the fal English voice |
| The retry | `observed < 0.6` triggers scaffold rung one; the retry scores at 0.6 weight because a scaffolded success is weaker evidence |
| "What changed?" | Learning-engine stage six; a self-report matching the detected error tag raises confidence in the estimate |
| Confidence 39% → 47% | An ordinary Profile Dimension — a bundle of delivery sub-competencies. Pause ratio, hesitation rate and mean turn length from fal Whisper word timestamps are the *evidence* that moves their mastery, so the number never bypasses the learner model |
| Sentence length 6 → 11 words | Verbatim word count over sentence count, both from the word-level transcript |
| **Day Three, different scene, same grammar** | `G006.01` retrievability drops below 0.85; the `sameTemplateAsLastTimeFor` penalty forces `EX018` instead of `EX001` |
| Week Two asks a harder question | Same domain, higher-CEFR sub-competencies now unlocked by the prerequisite graph |
| Presentation 22% → 59% | Path dimension for `university_success`, bundled over `F015.*` |
| Samuel's Complaint Handling 64% | Path dimension for `hospitality`, bundled over `F011.*` and `F020.*` |
| "The same engine" | Both learners run the same domains and libraries; only the Life Path skin differs |

## Demo script — five minutes

1. **Onboarding, 30 seconds.** Four questions. Point out that level is never asked.
2. **Assessment, 60 seconds.** Show the profile appear from four minutes of speech. Pre-record this; do not run it live.
3. **The mission, 90 seconds.** Hana speaks, the AI waits, the error is caught, the Amharic correction plays, she retries and succeeds.
4. **"What changed?", 20 seconds.** She names her own error. Say the words "immediate feedback, metacognition, spaced retrieval" here and nowhere else.
5. **Day Three, 90 seconds.** The payoff. New scene, new activity type, same grammar, scheduled automatically. This is the slide that wins it.
6. **Samuel, 30 seconds.** Same engine, hotel lobby. Close on Life Paths.

Two rules for the demo. Pre-record the audio round trips so a slow network can't kill the story. And never say "competency sub-ID" out loud — the judges should see Hana, and only find out afterwards that there was a curriculum engine underneath.
